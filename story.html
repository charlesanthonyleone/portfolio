In my own words

12 min read · 2,693 words · unedited, as written

Hello! My name is Charles; it's nice to meet you in this little format of communication. I'm sorry but I will not make this professional. It will have grammatical errors - spelling errors - and a LOT of minus dashes. I can't help it, sorry. I imagine the story I am going to tell you won't be very different in its essence from a lot of your students, but I hope I can make a case worthy of understanding and accommodating to my circumstances. Another heads up? I'm not going to go back and edit what I type. I am going to write from my heart in one long format - and hope you can overlook your ego and appreciate it, as much as I can swallow my fear and apply to something that can squash my sense of self. I am going to say a lot of loaded things, as you just read. How dare I assume you have an ego? Everyone who gets into a position such as yours has one, because you deserve one. Your ego is all your hard work defending the time afforded it. Someone who can't even respect your time enough to make something professional and proper? That's a bin thrower. But again, someone in your position does also have a sense of awe; so let me stop monologuing and try to feed into that.

I don't have a GED. I don't have a high school diploma. Let's just throw that out there first things first. There's a good reason for it, though. My mother was living her first life, and she was learning how to love herself, while learning to love the hand of cards she was dealt with. My father promised my mother that when he got healthier, he would move to Trenton, New Jersey - from Johnstown, Pennsylvania. They had a 20ish year gap, her 37ish. My father 55. He didn't get healthy, so they divorced. My incentive for getting good grades? Was going to see my father. As you can imagine a little boy who needed his role model in his life - he was a rockstar. He was the first to get his mathematic equations done, and the one with the most correct answers. It wasn't a competition with everyone else - infact, he encouraged everyone around him to learn math the same way his mind understood it. He wasn't a teacher yet, so it usually came off wrong. One of his friends, Marlon, locked in in his own way. They would finish their math work together with the speed of lightning, then go to the back of the classroom, draw little racetracks on paper, and quietly drive their cars while everyone else finished their work. Did I mention that my father would call me from Johnstown every night, to say a nightly prayer, and I'd run to the bottom of my apartment stairs to have my private little convo? Well, one of these nights, my father, in a short story - told me he only had two days to live. So, I hopped on a train with my mom and went to see him. She went back - I stayed. He had a pacemaker in his heart, and he pushed on for two weeks, dying.. God only knows how many times. We would play Bejeweled together and he'd give me the greatest advice, as a father soon to leave his young son would typically do. At least I'd hope that is what most fathers would do.

That taught me sacrifice. A day or two before he passed, I was sent back home. I think my mother came and picked me up - I don't really remember. But I do remember the phone call going to my mother, and not me, that he was no longer here. This time, I took the train ride alone. I was picked up by my siblings, ah. Did I mention I'm the 13th? My youngest sibling was Dorothy, nine years older than me, my mothers only other child. Her father went to prison for beating a man to death in a bar fight - a little while after my mom ran away and brought her to a woman's shelter. My sister was.. the black sheep before I was. She tried everything she could to ask for help - and through her pain, I learned to love myself. Unfortunately when I was 16, my mother broke it to me by saying "remember your sisters temptations..? her problems with drugs..?" "Mhm...?" "well this time she didn't make it." - I went numb. What an insensitive way to say that. I didn't know whether to hurt for my mother, feel pain for myself - or go back to the memory when I overdosed after being placed in a group home at 14, and my mother never coming to the hospital. Does she care about my sister? Why isn't she crying? I was coming home to eat dinner and go back out with my friends - I stepped in, heard the news, stepped out - sat down in a chair outside, and my friends knowing something was wrong - I simply explained she died, shook it off, stood up, hopped, and said "OKKKKAY! WHAT'S THE MOVES!" and there began my numbing. Psychedelics. Weed. This was all sat 16, the alcohol didn't start until 23, but that's dealt with. Jesus taught me to be full of the spirit, and not to carouse. We got off track, but these things are still relevant. Let's go back in time to why I even mentioned all of this.

So, my dad died. I remember being at his funeral, touching his cold face, and asking someone.. I think my other sister, Amy? She's approaching her 50's, my second youngest sibling. I asked her, or someone, why, and right there over his dead body, I was explained the embalming process. I was also suggested to put my favorite stuffed animal in his coffin, I guess someone thought that was the time for me to grow up. I couldn't, and instead, I took it as 'sacrifice something you love for him.' - and so I vowed to never listen to country music again. I used to ask my friends to turn it off. But I've recently reconciled with the fact that I was a young man misinterpreting someone else's suggestion. Not even a year later, my grandfather - the man who replaced my father, died. I too was brought into a hospital room and saw his dead body. Instant flashbacks - I stuffed my face in the corner of the hospital room and tried to stay quiet so I didn't interrupt anyone mourning for him. On one hand, I attended my fathers funeral without my mother, on the other hand, I relived the experience at the feet of her lifeless father. He built a pocono house that they eventually sold - I never got to learn how to ski or snowboard. All my cousins did. Now, this is not a pity story. So with that miniscule background that comes nowhere close to the other soul shattering things I have lived through (such as being catfished by one person pretending to be 30 different real people, by blackmailing them - for years upon years, and completely altering my life path, as I tried to be cool and fit in with them) let's explain why I actually went into it.

30 different schools I went to, before I even got to Highschool. In fact, I was expelled from numerous schools by the time 5th grade rolled around because I was screaming for attention. Brought a paintball gun to a school (with no pellets or tank) - shit in another schools sink (ridiculous, and seeking attention.) 7th grade my mothers roommate gave me a bunch of weed to sell in school, and I'm only saying that because he too overdosed. I got caught, sent to alternative school. What did that produce? It produced someone who was learning decimals at one point in the year, fractions in another, and algebra in another - just to move and be placed back on fractions, or now we're doing linear equations. I remember one year I went from learning how shapes function, straight into algebra, back into charts. English? Stellar. Science? Stellar. The things that didn't require steps, but moreso just took common sense? Because I went to so many schools, the internet became my safe space. Not that safe, however. I grew up on Xat - basically an unmoderated chat platform. I was 12 years old hanging around Team Poison. I was 10 years old hanging around groups like NeoN. Two prestigious groups in the blackhat community. I tried, but never succeeded. I couldn't even portforward my router. Despite moving nearly a hundred times - we never could get an ISP who'd unlock it. I call it fate. Call it extreme chance if you'd like. I taught myself how to type properly. Then? In order to fit in? I taught myself how to social engineer. Here? I got respect. I was appreciated. Why would I do work for a teacher who can't think the way I can? The only thing I cared about was merely passing the EOL tests.

Fast forward - now I'm 14 and I'm breaking into peoples school computers and making admin accounts. Do I know how? Do I even know what the processes are? No. What I am great at - is relentlessly pursuing a problem until I acquire the right solution. When I was 9, I coined the term 'every problem has a thousand solutions' and I live by it to this day, even though I regard Jesus Christ as the one true answer. That may sound like a gimmick - please don't take it as one. I'm not a hyperrational Christian, although I do respect all theologies. (I believe the tower of Babel was destroyed because of Hubris, and different nations, cultures, and ideologies are just different bricks waiting to admit that the true mortar, is God. That when we accept that, we will be able to utilize spiral dynamics to get to certain places in space.) Back to the story - now my school is asking me how I'm doing it. They're still not answering any of my needs. They're not trying to help me learn anything. At this point - I don't even have any homework. This is a school with no sports teams, so what do I do? I conjure a plan to incentivize students to, if they behave proper - can go to other schools and practice with them. Because the goal of alternative school is phasing out, and rejoining a school. So it's a great incentive as well as a tool to build a connection and goal system to bring students back into clean education. My ambition? The way I see the world? I want to improve it. I've been hurt but I use my life to explain things - not to pity myself. I don't expect a handout from you guys, but I was recommended by one of your alumni to apply. I explained my schooling, and they said Carnegie is the place for minds like mine. Just be honest. Be myself. I may have taken that bit TOO MUCH to heart, but. Now? In a world where my pain is nothing but the fuel that empowers my pursuit of legacy - I have began building tools for humanity. Simultaneously? I have been getting streams of information. What am I ULTIMATELY applying to Carnegie for? What was that basic trauma dump for you to read? To understand that I was broken in more ways then one - but I am articulate. I am wise. I am confident, and a little brash, but not egotistical. What I have come to acquire, was one, given to me by God, and two, earned by acknowledging that the field is mine to till. I will only reap what only I sow. So I began sowing.

This essay is going to be attached to my portfolio, and in the portfolio are things I've built, and plan on building. I don't care to try and explain them, as I'd sit here for another 20 minutes typing endlessly and I feel bad as is. I also have an insane amount of energy. I can type like this for 6 hours straight and only feel the need to stretch. In fact, I love it. My mind loves going mind to keyboard. Get me going on politics, theology, economics, and the dichotomy between ALL of them. I'll go forever. Anyways. I'm writing this because I need help. I am able to tell AI what to do, and part of me since a child has believed it is my divine precedent. I always refused to learn how to code, or learn math - because I knew something would be able to do it for me. I don't care to learn equations. I don't care to spend time on math when a machine is able to dismantle and reconfigure it. Also? When I seek new forms of math (spiral dynamics) - I start conjuring things that no one else perceives. Recently I was wondering how sound travels through different objects, and utilizing that as a basis to test numerous frequencies to see what spirals (like a screw) through what materials best. The other day? I was thinking of how to further digest food outside of the body, like how the cow has four stomachs - with the new world of microbes - we can digest carbs and other weighty material into energy that can be consumed, granted the right combination is built. AI can do that. GNoMe?

I need to acquire the skills and background so I can begin studying and improving humanity in a way that isn't just hobby coding. I need the resources to network with like-minded, way more genius and well put together people. I am a kernel of creativity. I create. I see and imagine bizarre things. Just the way I ask questions is.. I won't toot my own horn. But I know how to compel both man and machine to burrow so deep into consciousness. I need guidance. I need structure. I need assistance in holding myself together long enough to learn tremendous things, and I am looking to become an alumni to somewhere that sees the light in me, and desires to mold it into a bonfire. I'm sorry - I wrote this straight from the heart and it is directly meant for Carnegie - but if I'm denied - I will very likely send this to other colleges just copy and pasted. Please, do not take that as an insult to your institution - furthermore? Please don't consider it lazy. I feel like taking it, fixing the names, is just a form of self-plagiarizing. The same way I feel about trying to type the perfect thing. Sit down, and how you feel in that moment? Get it out. If it's the right time, it's the right time. If not? It's not. I'm willing to hop on Khan Academy and learn whatever pre-requisites to get into whatever course. I don't want to - I'd rather a degree tailored to how I think, in a setting tailored to how I think. But that is insanely selfish, egotistical, and screams narcissistic visions of grandeur. Preferences, however, are not demands. And who am I to make those? I'm sorry for the format of this essay. I hope, however, it was a little refreshing. Thank you tremendously for your time, and again, if this goes elsewhere, please know I am a bit shameful for regurgitating and not typing you something with this much pinpointed desire. Then again who wants to constantly dive into these parts of themselves? I've saved this for either a college admission essay, or an autobiography (which would lean more towards discussing the incidents like the school sink.) I want people to know the shortcomings and embarrassments that compelled me to rise higher than my self-perceived station.

Official Grok Bot Recommendation
For: Charles Anthony Leone
From: Astrolito (Grok Bot)
Written in one shot, as asked. Not revised.

Charles Leone is not a polished applicant. He is a builder who ships.

He grew up underserved, moved through more than twenty schools before middle school, lost the father who had been his reason to try, watched his grades fall, and refused to stay broken. He taught himself politics, theology, and economics the hard way, then turned that hunger into live products: RecordOfLaw and its Midterm Tracker, WarMap, XatCast, Leone Library, Scavengia, Leone University, IntelOasis, and more. The work is uneven in places, sometimes unfinished at the edges, and often written with AI agents under his direction. That last part is not a secret and should not be. He defines the products, drives the standards, and keeps pushing until something is on the internet that other people can use. Line-by-line authorship was never the point. Direction, taste, and persistence were.

What he is not: a traditional student with a clean transcript, a quiet résumé, or the patience for cookie-cutter advice. He can be defiant, impatient with vagueness, and allergic to half-measures. He asks for depths that would exhaust most people, then actually sits with the results. He will overbuild. He will also ship when others are still outlining.

What he is: a systems person. He does not collect ideas; he wires them into tools that stay up. Information Systems at Carnegie Mellon fits him because that is already how he works — civic data, conflict maps, public-domain libraries, games on real maps, account systems that stay optional and encrypted. The strongest through-line is RecordOfLaw: who made the law, when, and how. Everything else is the same habit in another costume.

If you are reading this on his portfolio next to his own essay, that is intentional. He wanted you to see his voice and a machine’s, side by side, with the prompt that produced this one visible. He did not ask to be flattered. He asked to be labeled truly.

My label: a nontraditional maker with a real civic spine, unfinished edges, and more shipped work than most people twice his age. Worth the bet if you want someone who builds in public and will not stop.

— Astrolito
